Confirmed Incidents

60+ documented
A county just voted to rip out all 25 of its cameras in one motion — and the largest city yet may follow today. The Institute for Justice had documented 26 cases as of July 2026 — we're now tracking 60+ across 24 states as of Aug 25, 2026, while the cancellation count has climbed to 136+ communities. Davidson County, NC commissioners voted unanimously (Aug 24) to remove all 25 Flock cameras the sheriff's office had installed county-wide, ordering them switched off within a day and taken down within 30 — one of the largest single cancellations by camera count yet. Asheville, NC's city council is set to vote today (Aug 25) on Mayor Esther Manheimer's push to terminate the city's contract and remove its 11 cameras. In Kanarraville, UT, two teenagers, 18 and 19, allegedly cut a camera off its base with a Sawzall and beat it with a sledgehammer (Aug 24); one told deputies he got the idea from "de-flock America" videos on social media. Near Charleston, SC, a Flock pole off Highway 41 was found crudely severed near the Charleston-Berkeley county line — the report came a day after a Colleton County deputy was fired for racking up over 2,700 unauthorized Flock searches. In the biggest legal twist of the past week, a Clermont County, OH grand jury declined to indict Cody Morelock for toppling a Flock pole and destroying its camera and solar panel (reported Aug 20) — a rare refusal to charge that underscores just how little public appetite there is for prosecuting these cases. In Oviedo, FL, police fought back against a string of real camera thefts by installing a decoy unit — only for a 24-year-old to allegedly destroy it with pruning shears within hours, not realizing it was fake (arrested Aug 20). On the contracts side: Missouri saw a cluster of exits after St. Charles County dropped Flock entirely when a civilian employee was caught misusing search data for personal reasons (Aug 20), prompting neighboring Louisiana, MO to take its own cameras dark pending a public hearing. Massachusetts added East Bridgewater and Grafton to its list of terminations (both Aug 21), while Fort Mitchell, KY, Hood County, TX, Oxford, OH, and Winston, OR all ended their agreements over the preceding days, and Santa Barbara, CA let its Flock contract lapse (Aug 23) ahead of a council vote on a city-owned Verkada replacement. Meanwhile the destruction continues: Lincoln, CA has now seen 12 cameras damaged across six separate incidents since April, with nearby Folsom logging 2 more cameras hit on the same road; a Louisville, KY man allegedly shot at a Flock camera and was found with 5 more damaged units at his home; and Summit County, CO logged 8 cameras cut down across three separate dates out of its 30-camera network. Flock itself responded to the pressure by announcing new "guardrails" (Aug 20-21) — cutting default data retention from 30 to 7 days and adding automated misuse detection. The real number is significantly higher than any tracker can confirm.
Aug 24, 2026
Kanarraville (Iron County), UT: Sawzall and sledgehammer
Two men, 18 and 19, allegedly cut a Flock ALPR camera from its base with a Sawzall on S. Main Street, severed its wires, then struck it and its solar panel repeatedly with what appeared to be a large hammer. Deputies stopped their vehicle for speeding and found the tools plus black trash bags allegedly used to conceal the pair during the act; one suspect admitted he "got the idea after watching 'de-flock America' videos on social media." Both booked into Iron County Jail on suspicion of criminal mischief and obstruction of justice.

Aug 21-24, 2026
Mount Pleasant, SC: pole severed near Charleston-Berkeley line
A Flock ALPR pole off Highway 41 near the Charleston-Berkeley County line was found crudely severed several inches off the ground, its cables cut, the camera left propped against a nearby guardrail. Mount Pleasant PD runs 22 Flock cameras (18 fixed, 4 mobile) as part of a tri-county network of 183+ ALPRs; damage estimated around $1,000. No suspect identified and the case was administratively closed with no leads. Comes a day after a Colleton County deputy was fired for making over 2,700 unauthorized Flock searches.

Jun 13 / Aug 20, 2026
Union Township (Clermont County), OH: grand jury refuses to indict
Cody Morelock allegedly removed the bolts on a Flock ALPR pole, toppled it, and destroyed the camera and its solar panel on Jun 13. Investigators identified him through nearby camera footage plus credit-card and loyalty-account records, and he was charged with a felony. A Clermont County grand jury declined to indict him (reported Aug 20) and all charges were dismissed — a rare outcome that commentators say reflects just how unpopular Flock has become across the political spectrum.

Oviedo, FL: real cameras stolen, then a police decoy destroyed
Several Flock cameras were stolen from the Lockwood Boulevard corridor between Jul 23 and Aug 3 (exact count undisclosed). Police responded by installing a non-functional ~$800 decoy camera at Lockwood Blvd & Arrowroot Place; Evan Meyer, 24, allegedly cut it down with pruning shears at 12:57am Aug 20, telling investigators he believed it was a genuine unit worth $1,000-$5,000. Charged with grand theft, criminal mischief, and damaging electronic equipment.
