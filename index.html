Champlin, MN: vandals hit two camera sites, city pulls its entire fleet
Champlin took every one of its Flock license plate readers offline after vandals struck equipment at two camera locations, city officials confirmed in a Facebook post. The city is now operating zero Flock cameras while staff compile formal answers to resident questions on installation dates, data collection, retention, and program cost, due in the Sept. 14 city council packet. Part of a broader Minnesota retreat that also includes Sherburne and Isanti counties stepping away from their own Flock trials.

Isanti County, MN: 2 of 8 camera poles cut down, sheriff ends trial
Two of the Isanti County Sheriff's Office's eight Flock camera poles were cut down during the department's trial period. Sheriff Wayne Seiberlich ended the trial by the end of August — saying the vandalism "is in no way a political victory" but citing the $3,000-per-camera cost against other budget priorities as the deciding factor — after the cameras had helped solve an arson case and locate a missing person during the trial.

Burnet County, TX: task force pulls every fixed camera after vandalism
The Heart of Texas Auto Theft Task Force removed all fixed Flock license plate readers it operated in Burnet County after vandals disabled two of them in separate incidents — one struck with a blunt object with its battery smashed and cables cut, the other had its mounting brackets and power cables cut. The task force, funded through Texas DMV grants, covers 4,702 square miles across Burnet, Coryell, McLennan, and Williamson counties; Flock cameras run separately by municipal police in the Highland Lakes area were unaffected. Criminal investigations are open in both cases with no suspects identified, and the removal follows residents demanding the county terminate its Flock contract entirely at an earlier commissioners court meeting.
