Location undisclosed: hacker collective dismantles a Flock camera, exposes its internal data
A hacker collective calling itself stegan0gram says it pulled a Flock Safety camera out of the field, dismantled it, and extracted a near-complete copy of its stored data — including an unencrypted "media" partition holding the on-device encryption key Flock said protected footage — before sharing its findings with 404 Media, Distributed Denial of Secrets, and Wired. The group says the single camera captured 50,200 vehicles and generated 1.6 million photos in just 21 days, and that it could pick out details as small as an American-flag patch on a motorcycle saddlebag. Flock called the "unauthorized removal and tampering" illegal; the collective declined to disclose the camera's location, citing retaliation concerns.
